# Anachronism checklist: a legionary at Alesia, 52 BC

Each item cites sources.json. Where the open evidence reached is older or newer than 52 BC, it says so.

- Shield: the large curved scutum with an iron boss, carried with a leather cover on the march [s1 6.23.2-5, s2 2.21]. A flat rectangular "Hollywood" shield is a guess; the shape in 52 BC is not shown by any source reached here.
- Sword: the Spanish sword (gladius hispaniensis) worn on the right thigh [s1 6.23.6]; a 3rd-1st century BC example survives [s4]. The shorter Pompeii-type gladius belongs to the 1st century AD and is out of period.
- Javelins: two pila [s1 6.23.8-11].
- Helmet: bronze ("brass" in Paton's translation), crested with three upright purple or black feathers in Polybius's day [s1 6.23.8, 6.23.12]; the Montefortino type is the open-access object nearest in form, but the Met example is two centuries earlier [s3].
- Body armour: a small square heart-protector for most men in Polybius's time, mail for the wealthy [s1 6.23.14-15]. Whether all of Caesar's legionaries wore mail is not settled by these sources. Segmented plate armour (lorica segmentata) is not attested by any source here and belongs to the imperial period: leave it out.
- Dating gap: Polybius wrote about a century before Alesia; Caesar confirms helmets, shield covers and badges for 57 BC [s2].
